It all begins with your future plans for the yard

Your plans for the space can influence the pool removal approach. Lawn and landscaping may require a different level of preparation than paving or future construction, including decisions about shell removal, backfilling and project documentation.

Key factors influencing project scope

Every site has different conditions. Pool type and depth, nearby paving, access, retaining walls, services, groundwater and the amount of material to be removed can all affect the work required. Narrow access can also limit equipment options and increase labour requirements.

A responsible removal plan

Pool removal involves more than breaking up the pool. The work should account for water, services, filtration equipment, waste disposal, drainage, fill material, compaction and final levels, along with any approval, certification or engineering requirements that apply before work starts.

Next steps after pool removal

After the pool is removed, the area can be finished with simple turf or incorporated into a larger backyard project. Options may include planting, paving, drainage improvements or an outdoor entertaining area. Planning these works before removal can minimise duplicated site work and access costs.

What can have the biggest impact on cost

Several key elements shape the final cost: the pool's construction type, shell volume, concrete thickness, reinforcement, alongside site access, groundwater management, tipping fees, fill material volume, surrounding paving, and your intended level of surface reinstatement.

Full removal vs partial removal

Removing the entire pool generally costs more because there is more material to excavate, handle and remove from site. Partial removal may reduce the price where both the remaining structure and intended future use are suitable.

What should be included in a quote

A pool removal quote should clearly outline whether it covers water removal, service isolation, demolition, disposal fees, the type and quantity of fill, placement and compaction, drainage, topsoil, turf, required approvals and site clean-up.

Why a Site Visit Is Important

Initial project details can often be reviewed from photos, but some site conditions are difficult to assess remotely. Access, levels, structures and services may need to be checked in person before a firm quote can be provided, with specialist advice required in some cases.

Approvals & licensing

Do I need building or council approval to remove a pool?

Potentially, yes. QBCC guidance states that a pool must be lawfully decommissioned and that building approval may be required. It also recommends checking with your local council before work begins. For properties in Brisbane or on the Gold Coast, confirm the approval requirements with the relevant council and a licensed private building certifier before demolition or filling starts. When can a pool be removed from the Queensland pool register?

Once a property no longer has a regulated pool, the owner can ask for the pool to be removed from the pool register in writing, in line with QBCC requirements. The pool must have been lawfully decommissioned and must not be capable of holding more than 300 millimetres of water. [Owner] Not independently verified

Methods & materials

Can I simply fill in an old swimming pool with dirt?

Pool filling is more than simply bringing in dirt. The decommissioning method should consider drainage, which parts of the pool shell remain, suitable fill material, staged placement, compaction, final levels and how the area will be used in the future.

What is the difference between complete pool removal and partial pool removal?

Complete removal involves removing the pool shell or structure and disposing of the demolition waste before backfilling. Partial removal leaves part of the structure below ground after selected sections are demolished. Your future site plan is an important factor when choosing between the two.

What materials are used to backfill a swimming pool?

The right backfill material depends on the approved removal method, site conditions and how the area will be used in the future. Clean fill, sand, gravel or engineered fill may be suitable in different situations. The material should be placed using an appropriate method, with drainage and compaction considered as part of the work.

What happens to the concrete, steel and fibreglass after demolition?

Concrete, steel, fibreglass and other demolition materials should be handled and disposed of through appropriate facilities. For complete pool removal, the shell and associated debris are normally carted away rather than burying uncontrolled demolition waste in the former pool excavation.

Process & timing

How long does the pool removal process take from start to finish?

Most residential pool removal projects take a few working days, but the timeframe can vary depending on the pool construction, site access, concrete volume, machinery and truck access, approvals, weather, backfilling and landscaping. Limited access or heavily reinforced concrete may add to the project time.

How are pool pumps, electrics, and plumbing handled during removal?

As part of the project, pool equipment and services need to be safely disconnected or removed. Electrical, plumbing and drainage work should be undertaken by appropriately licensed trades where required. When discussing the quote, tell us about any pumps, heaters, lighting, chlorinators and nearby services.

What information is needed for a pool removal quote?

Please provide your suburb, pool type, approximate length, width and depth, clear photos, the narrowest access width, nearby retaining walls or buildings, and how you plan to use the area afterwards. This helps us assess machinery, demolition, filling and reinstatement requirements ahead of a site inspection.

Site & access

Can a pool be removed with tight or restricted site access?

In most cases, yes. Restricted side access may require smaller excavators, mini loaders, tracked dumpers, more manual handling, or extra cartage. Please measure the narrowest point from the street to the pool and attach site photos with your enquiry.

Is a concrete pool harder to remove than a fibreglass pool?

The removal process is different for each type. Concrete pools may involve breaking up reinforced concrete and separating steel for disposal. Fibreglass shells can often be cut, lifted or broken into sections that can be transported. Site access, pool depth, coping and nearby structures can have as much impact on the work as the pool material itself.

Do you remove spas, plunge pools and above-ground pools?

Yes. We can provide quotes for spas, plunge pools and above-ground pools. The removal process and any approval requirements will depend on how the structure is installed and whether it is designed to hold 300 millimetres or more of water. [Owner] Not independently verified

After removal & future building

Can I build over a removed or filled-in pool?

Potentially, but this needs to be considered before the pool is removed. The outcome depends on the removal method, fill material, compaction, ground conditions, proposed building and engineering requirements. If you may add an extension, garage, shed or granny flat later, get geotechnical, structural and building certification advice before choosing a pool removal method.

Do I need a compaction certificate after pool removal?

It depends on your future plans for the site. A basic landscaped area may not require formal compaction documentation. If you plan to build on the site later, ask your certifier, engineer or geotechnical professional what controlled fill, testing and records should be provided and retained.

Will the ground settle after a pool is removed?

Ground settlement can occur if fill material is unsuitable, poorly placed or not properly compacted. Using appropriate fill, placing it in controlled layers, allowing for drainage and compacting it correctly can help reduce the risk. The required approach will also depend on how the area will be used, whether for lawn, paving or future construction.

Can the former pool area be finished with lawn, gardens, paving or a patio?

The former pool area can be restored as lawn, garden space, a play area or part of a wider landscaping project. Paving, patios and other structures may require additional planning for ground preparation, drainage, footings and approvals.
